## Artefact 2: Data-driven bottleneck identification

**Artefact type:** Peer-reviewed journal article  
**Source:** [Identification of Delays and Bottlenecks in Manufacturing Processes Through Process Mining](https://doi.org/10.56578/jii030202)

![Opening section of the process-mining bottleneck study by Turgay, Demir and Eryurur](assets/process-analysis/artefact-2.png)

*Figure 2. Source excerpt from the process-mining bottleneck study (Turgay, Demir & Eryürür 2025, p. 69).*

Turgay, Demir and Eryürür use event logs to reconstruct the actual process and compare it with the designed model. They use a combination of waiting time, queue size, resource use and deviation frequency in their method. This provides a wider perspective of bottlenecks than can be obtained from one performance measure (Turgay, Demir & Eryürür 2025, pp. 69, 72).

In particular, the study led me to consider that process data is not necessarily objective. The method also requires contextual knowledge when setting weights and thresholds (Turgay, Demir & Eryürür 2025, p. 85). Before accepting the model as accurate I would always assess the quality of the data and have a conversation with the individuals involved in the process.

## Artefact 3: Process mining in tunnel construction

**Artefact type:** Applied case study  
**Source:** [A case study on integrating data analysis and process mining in conventional tunnel construction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dibe.2025.100640)

![CRISP-DM cycle used to analyse tunnel construction data](assets/process-analysis/artefact-3.png)

*Figure 3. CRISP-DM approach used for tunnel construction data analysis (Melnyk et al. 2025, p. 6).*

Based on construction records, Melnyk et al. analyse the sequences and durations of tunnel activity and the progress of the tunnel construction works. Process discovery and conformance checking show that there are gaps between the planned and observed work. Surprisingly, actual progress was about 20 per cent faster than estimates based on literature (Melnyk et al. 2025, pp. 1, 12-13).

A manager might be under the impression of a delay when in fact there is better performance. But the research period was one month in one project. Some records were vague and miscellaneous, which reduced the detail of the analysis (Melnyk et al. 2025, p. 13). This limitation enabled me to realise that additional data can be valuable if activities are captured in a uniform and appropriate manner.

## Artefact 4: Pareto and fishbone analysis of procurement delays

**Artefact type:** Professional research report  
**Source:** [Spare Parts Acquisition in the Sri Lanka Navy](https://dair.nps.edu/bitstream/123456789/5464/1/NPS-AM-26-015.pdf)

![Cover of the Sri Lanka Navy procurement bottleneck report](assets/process-analysis/artefact-4.png)

*Figure 4. Cover of the procurement bottleneck research report (Kathriarachchi 2025, p. i).*

Kathriarachchi discusses procurement delays in the Sri Lanka Navy. Pareto analysis determines the stages where files are languishing. Causes are then explored with fishbone diagrams and Five Whys. The problems identified are supplier fulfilment, supplier selection, quality acceptance and budget commitment (Kathriarachchi 2025, pp. 27-30).

Possible explanations are organised using fishbone and Five Whys. But the diagrams cannot prove that an assumed cause produced the delay. Interviews, records and performance data are still required. This relates to Week 3 because people, rules, handoffs and controls across the process should be considered in process analysis (Morshed 2026, slides 19-26). These tools would be a starting point and not the final answer.
